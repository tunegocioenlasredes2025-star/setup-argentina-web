FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about establishing and operating a company in Argentina.

Argentina offers several entity types for foreign investors. The most common are the SAS (Simplified Joint-Stock Company), ideal for startups and SMEs with a faster registration process; the SRL (LLC), suited for small to mid-size operations; and the SA (Corporation), preferred for larger ventures. We also handle branch registrations for companies that prefer to extend their existing foreign entity into Argentina. We'll recommend the best structure based on your goals, industry, and ownership setup.

Most company formations are completed within 30 to 60 business days from the moment all required documentation is submitted. The timeline depends on the entity type and government processing times at the IGJ. SAS entities tend to be faster. SetUP keeps you informed at every milestone so there are no surprises.

No. The entire process can be completed remotely from anywhere in the world. We handle all filings, notary coordination, and government submissions on your behalf. You'll need to provide notarized and apostilled documents from your home country — which we guide you through step by step.

The CUIT (Código Único de Identificación Tributaria) is Argentina's Tax Identification Number. Every company operating legally in Argentina must have a CUIT — it's required to open bank accounts, issue invoices, hire employees, and fulfill all tax obligations with ARCA. Obtaining the CUIT is the key milestone in the company formation process, and the moment our professional fee becomes due.

Once registered, your Argentine company will have monthly and annual obligations including: tax filings with ARCA, bookkeeping and financial statements, corporate record-keeping, and annual IGJ filings. Depending on your activity, additional regulatory filings may apply. SetUP's accounting and compliance service covers all of this so you stay fully up to date.

Yes — through our Employer of Record (EOR) service. We act as the legal employer for your Argentine-based staff while you maintain operational control. This allows you to compliantly hire local talent before completing your full company formation.

Government fees, notary costs, and official disbursements are separate from our professional fees and are billed at exact cost as they arise — with full transparency and receipts provided for every expense. We provide a full estimate at the outset so you can plan accordingly.

Argentine companies are subject to several taxes including Corporate Income Tax (35% on net profit), VAT (21% standard rate), Gross Revenue Tax (provincial — varies by activity), and others. The specific tax burden depends on your industry and corporate structure. Our tax advisory team will model your projected obligations during the initial consultation.

We work primarily in Spanish and English. Agustín and the SetUP team communicate directly with international clients in English — no intermediaries, no translation delays. All official documents are in Spanish as required by Argentine law, and we provide full explanations in English throughout the entire process. We also coordinate with Portuguese-speaking clients.
